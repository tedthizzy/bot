# Power system and thermal design: Pi 4 + ESP32-S3 + two gearmotors on 3S Li-ion

Research note, 2026-09-07. Scope: battery, rails, grounding, power budget, telemetry, thermal, clean shutdown. Tags: [MEASURED] someone ran it on named hardware, [VENDOR] spec sheet or vendor claim, [INFERRED] my estimate.

## Summary

- Keep the brief's 3S Li-ion 18650 choice. Build or buy a 3S2P pack of 3500 mAh energy cells (Samsung INR18650-35E class: 3500 mAh, 8 A continuous [VENDOR]) with a 3S 20 A balancing BMS, a 10 A blade fuse at the pack, and XT60 on the pack (XT30 on branch leads). Usable energy is about 60 Wh; the rover's average draw is about 12 W, so runtime is roughly 4-5 h [INFERRED]. A 3S1P pack still meets the 1-2 h target but leaves no margin for 5 A-stall motors.
- Three rails: (1) motor rail straight from the battery through the fuse and a series NC e-stop; (2) Pi rail from a Pololu D24V50F5 (6-38 V in, 5 A, $32.95) or D36V50F5 (5.5-50 V, 5.5 A, $39.95) [VENDOR]; (3) a separate small 5 V buck for the ESP32-S3, VL53L1X pair, and I2C sensors. The brief's $20 for the Pi buck is low; $10-class LM2596/XL4015 modules are the ones that brown out (realistic 1 A continuous, 5.05-5.2 V sag under 1.3 A load [MEASURED, forum]).
- Official Pi 4 numbers hold in 2026: 5.1 V at 3.0 A recommended, 1.2 A total for USB peripherals, 600 mA typical bare-board active current, low-voltage warning at 4.63 V (+/-5 %) [VENDOR]. The docs add "keep the supply above 4.8 V" [VENDOR].
- The Pi 4 has no input polyfuse. Header injection on pins 2/4 bypasses the USB-C TVS/overvoltage protection, not a fuse; add your own 3 A polyfuse and TVS. Voltage drop in the 5 V wiring is the dominant brown-out cause: a D36V50F5 measured 5.06 V at its terminals and 4.7-4.85 V at the Pi through DuPont leads; soldered thick wire fixed it to 4.96 V under load [MEASURED, Aug 2026].
- Thermal: Pi 4 throttles Arm clocks progressively between 80 and 85 C and Arm+GPU at 85 C; there is no soft limit on Pi 4 (`temp_soft_limit` is 3A+/3B+ only) [VENDOR]. A bare Pi 4 hits 82-83 C and throttles to 1000 MHz under 3-4 core stress at 22-23 C ambient; the closed official case reaches 88 C and 428 MHz [MEASURED]. Fit a heatsink plus a 30/40 mm 5 V fan driven by `dtoverlay=gpio-fan,gpiopin=14,temp=60000` and give the enclosure an air path.
- Telemetry and shutdown: INA226 high-side on the battery main lead with a 2-3 mOhm external shunt, read by the ESP32-S3 (the stock 0.1 Ohm shunt saturates the INA226 at 0.8 A). Per-cell thresholds under load: warn 3.5 V, stop driving 3.3 V, shut the Pi down 3.2 V, 10 s debounce. ESP32 asserts `gpio-shutdown`, waits for `gpio-poweroff` (default GPIO 26), then cuts the Pi rail with a P-MOSFET.

## State of the art (2026)

**Battery chemistry.** Hobby indoor rovers in the LeRobot ecosystem run 12 V-class Li-ion: the LeKiwi 12 V BOM lists a "12v 5a Battery" ($32.99) with a DC5521 plug and a "12v to 5v 5a USB-C converter" ($9.99) for the Pi; the 5 V variant uses a 20,000 mAh 85 W USB-PD power bank [VENDOR, SIGRobotics-UIUC BOM]. 3S Li-ion (11.1 V nominal, 12.6 V full) stays the default because chargers, BMS boards, and 12 V motor drivers are built for it. 4S LiFePO4 (12.8 V nominal, 14.2-14.6 V full, 10.0 V cutoff [VENDOR]) is safer and longer-lived, but exceeds the 13.5 V limit of TB6612FNG-class drivers [VENDOR, Pololu #713], needs a LiFePO4 BMS (3.65 V/cell charge cutoff), and its flat curve defeats voltage-based state of charge. 3S LiPo has C-rating headroom the rover cannot use, no built-in BMS, and needs a balance charger.

**Cells.** Energy cells rather than power cells: Samsung INR18650-35E is 3500 mAh, 3.6 V nominal, 8 A continuous, 4.2 V max, 2.5 V cutoff [VENDOR, imrbatteries.com]. 3S2P = 7.0 Ah, 16 A continuous, about 75 Wh nominal.

**BMS and charging.** Typical 3S 20 A BMS boards give overcharge, over-discharge (2.5-3.0 V/cell), overcurrent, short-circuit, and passive balancing. A BMS does not itself balance-charge; a 3S pack needs a 12.6 V CC/CV charger or a hobby balance charger (ISDT/ToolkitRC class) via a JST-XH lead [VENDOR, Zbotic/Alibaba guides 2025]. Cheap BMS cut off at 2.5 V/cell, so firmware must own the usable cutoff.

**Connectors.** A 2026 FPV guide rates XT30 at 30 A continuous / 45 A 10 s burst with 14-18 AWG and XT60 at 60 A / 100 A with 12-14 AWG; clean XT60 joints measure under 0.3 mOhm [VENDOR, uavmodel 2026-05-31]. Conservative suppliers rate XT30 at 15-18 A continuous [VENDOR, Zbotic].

**Pi 4 power path.** Official docs (raspberrypi/documentation, master, fetched 2026-09-07): Pi 4 Model B recommended PSU 3.0 A, max USB peripheral draw 1.2 A, typical bare-board active current 600 mA; Raspberry Pi measured (June 2019 OS, Ethernet, HDMI, keyboard, mouse) boot max 0.85 A, idle 0.6 A, H.264 playback 0.78-0.85 A, stress avg 1.2 A / max 1.25 A, halt 0.023 A [MEASURED, vendor lab]. Low-voltage detection trips below 4.63 V (+/-5 %) and logs to the kernel [VENDOR]. Camera Module 250 mA, HDMI 50 mA, GPIO 50 mA total [VENDOR]. The Pi 4 board has no polyfuse; the USB-C input has an SMBJ TVS for overvoltage/reverse protection; powering via header 5 V pins works (the official PoE HAT does it) but bypasses that protection, so add a 3 A polyfuse [forum consensus, davidcoton/joan, INFERRED from schematic]. Independent bench: 575 mA idle, 885 mA LXDE load peak, 640 mA recording 1080p with Camera v1, 69 mA powered down, all at 4.965 V input [MEASURED, raspi.tv 2019, clamp meter]. Firmware updates by Nov 2019 cut idle from 2.89 W to 2.1 W and full load from 7.28 W to 6.41 W [MEASURED, Raspberry Pi blog].

**5 V regulators.** Pololu's D24V50F5 (6-38 V in, 5 A, 85-95 % efficiency, <1 mA quiescent, $32.95) and D36V50F5 (5.5-50 V, 5.5 A, 4 mA quiescent, reverse protection to -40 V, $39.95) are the standard hobby-robotics parts; the D24V90F5 (9 A) and D42V55F5 (6 A) exist for bigger loads [VENDOR]. Pololu support's Aug 2026 diagnosis of a Pi 5 under-voltage case is the useful 2026 datapoint: the regulator held 5.06-5.07 V but the Pi read 4.7-4.85 V through DuPont jumpers; soldered thicker wire gave 4.96-4.97 V under load and >5.0 V idle [MEASURED]. LM2596 modules: 5.9 V no-load and 5.05-5.2 V under 1.3 A, too hot to touch, "realistically ~1 A continuous", 20 of 20 modules failed one user [MEASURED, forum 2017].

**ESP32-S3.** Datasheet v2.2, 3.3 V, 25 C: Wi-Fi TX peak 340 mA (802.11b 21 dBm), 291 mA (g), 283-286 mA (n); RX 88-91 mA; modem-sleep with dual cores busy at 160 MHz 49.6-64.1 mA [VENDOR]. ESP-IDF v6.1 measurement guide shows 23.88 mA active for an ESP32-S3-WROOM-1 in its test [MEASURED]. With Wi-Fi off (serial to the Pi) budget 60-100 mA.

**Sensors and audio.** VL53L1X carrier: 20 mA typical ranging, 40 mA peak, 2.6-5.5 V in [VENDOR, Pololu #3415]. MAX98357A I2S amp: 3.2 W at 4 Ohm / 1.8 W at 8 Ohm at 5 V, bridge-tied output with no ground reference, $5.95 [VENDOR, Adafruit #3006].

**Motors and drivers.** JGA25-370 12 V 130 rpm encoder motor: rated 0.3 A, stall 1.8 A, no-load 0.07 A [VENDOR]. Pololu 25D HP 12 V 20.4:1 with encoder: free-run 0.30 A, stall 5.0 A, max-efficiency point 0.88 A, $56.95 [VENDOR]. TB6612FNG carrier: 4.5-13.5 V, 1 A continuous / 3 A peak per channel, $4.95, "can get hot enough to burn you long before the chip overheats" [VENDOR]. Adafruit DRV8871: 6.5-45 V, 3.6 A peak, current limit about 2 A with the stock 30 kOhm resistor, thermal shutdown, $7.50 [VENDOR].

**Thermal.** Official: Arm cores progressively throttled between 80 and 85 C, Arm+GPU at 85 C; DVFS steps 1500/1000/750/600 MHz; Pi 4 "currently no soft limit defined"; `temp_limit` defaults to 85 and clamps at 85 [VENDOR]. Martin Rowan (Sept 2019, 22-23 C ambient, `stress`, frequency from `vcgencmd measure_clock arm`): bare board 1/2/3/4 cores 69/76/82/83 C with 1000 MHz throttling at 3-4 cores; official closed case 83/86/87/88 C and 1000/600/500/428 MHz; Flirc peaked 73 C with no throttling; Fan Shim inside the official case 64/71/75/82 C [MEASURED]. He also found `/sys/.../scaling_cur_freq` did not show throttling; only `vcgencmd measure_clock arm` did. The official Case Fan defaults to switching on at 80 C on GPIO 14 with 1.4 CFM [VENDOR]; the `gpio-fan` overlay defaults are gpiopin 12, temp 55000, hyst 10000 [VENDOR, overlays README].

**Telemetry.** INA219: 12-bit, 26 V, +/-320 mV shunt range. INA226: 16-bit, 36 V, +/-81.92 mV shunt range, averaging up to 1024 samples, alert pin, 16 addresses [VENDOR]. With the stock 0.1 Ohm shunt the INA226 saturates at about 0.8 A and the shunt dissipates 10 W at 10 A; use high-side sensing and a low-value external shunt [Hackaday.io project, Dec 2025]. RobTillaart/INA226 (Arduino) supports `setMaxCurrentShunt()`, averaging, and alert masks.

**Shutdown.** `gpio-shutdown` generates KEY_POWER on a GPIO (default GPIO3, which conflicts with I2C); `gpio-poweroff` (default GPIO 26) drives the pin active for 100 ms, inactive 100 ms, then active at poweroff, disables GPIO3 wake, and requires an external circuit to cut power [VENDOR, overlays README]. Bootloader: `WAKE_ON_GPIO=1` (default) keeps a low-power halt woken by GPIO3/GLOBAL_EN; `POWER_OFF_ON_HALT=1` with `WAKE_ON_GPIO=0` switches off all PMIC outputs but 5 V stays on and GLOBAL_EN must be grounded to boot [VENDOR; rpi-eeprom 2711 latest release 2026-08-04]. The zbchristian Raspi_PowerSwitch pattern: MCU pulses GPIO 17 for shutdown, watches GPIO 27 as "running", then opens a MOSFET after a timeout. UPS HATs: Geekworm X728 (5.1 V 5 A, 2x18650, $45) and Waveshare UPS HAT (E) (5 V 6 A, 4x21700, INA219, power-off 60 s after <5 %) [VENDOR].

## Recommendation for this build

**Pack.** 3S2P Samsung 35E (or LG MJ1/Molicel P28A class), spot-welded or in a 6-cell holder, 3S 20 A BMS with balance leads, JST-XH balance connector left accessible, XT60 main lead, 10 A mini-blade fuse on the positive lead inside the pack sleeve. If motors are JGA25-370 class (1.8 A stall) a 3S1P pack and 7.5 A fuse are enough; if 25D HP (5.0 A stall each) use 2P and 15 A. Buying instead: the LeKiwi-style "12 V 5 A" Li-ion pack with integrated BMS and DC5521 is the path of least resistance, then add an XT60 pigtail.

**Charging.** Onboard BMS plus a 12.6 V 2 A CC/CV wall charger into a panel DC5521 jack; the rover charges docked with the e-stop pressed. Keep the balance lead so a hobby balance charger can rebalance every 10-20 cycles if cell spread exceeds 50 mV.

**Rails.**
1. Motor rail: battery -> fuse -> NC mushroom e-stop (rated >=10 A DC) -> motor drivers. Nothing else on this rail. Wire the e-stop's aux contact to an ESP32 input so firmware knows.
2. Pi rail: Pololu D24V50F5 (or D36V50F5), input taken at the fuse output, not at the e-stop, so the Pi stays up when the e-stop is pressed. 470-1000 uF electrolytic (25 V) at the buck input, 18 AWG output leads under 15 cm, soldered, no DuPont. Inject at header pins 2/4 through a 3 A polyfuse plus an SMBJ5.0A TVS, or use a short USB-C pigtail if you want the board's own protection; measure >=5.0 V at the header under full load, and treat 4.8 V as a hard floor [VENDOR].
3. Logic rail: a second small 5 V buck (1-2 A) for ESP32-S3, 2x VL53L1X, INA226, bumpers. Motor driver logic pins take their VCC from here too.

**Drivers.** Two Adafruit DRV8871 (6.5-45 V, 2 A limit, 3.6 A peak) or a dual DRV8876/TB67H420 board. Keep TB6612FNG only for JGA25-class motors and only on 3S. Mount drivers where the fan's exhaust passes; at 1 A per bridge a DRV8871 dissipates about 1.1 W (two 565 mOhm FETs in the path) and about 4.5 W at its 2 A limit [INFERRED from datasheet Rds(on)].

**Grounding and noise.** Single star point at the power distribution board's battery negative; each buck's ground and the motor driver ground return to it separately. 0.1 uF ceramics across each motor and from each terminal to the can, twisted motor leads, motor wiring routed away from I2C/UART/mic cables [VENDOR, Pololu]. I2C pull-ups 2.2-3.3 kOhm on the 3.3 V bus, bus under 20 cm, or move the ToF sensors to the ESP32 where they belong anyway. Audio: use the I2S MAX98357A on the Pi rail (bridge-tied, no ground path to the speaker), not the Pi 4's 3.5 mm PWM output. USB mic: power from the Pi's USB (same ground), keep its cable away from motor leads; if hum remains, a full-speed USB isolator (ADuM3160 class) or a mic module on the ESP32 side. On battery there is one ground, so remaining hum is inductive coupling, not a mains ground loop [INFERRED].

Runtime: 5 V loads 1.6 A x 5.1 V / 0.88 efficiency = 9.3 W from the battery; motors 2 x 0.45 A x 11 V at 40 % duty = 4 W; total about 13 W. 3S2P 35E: 7.0 Ah x 10.8 V average = 75 Wh, 80 % usable above 3.3 V/cell = 60 Wh -> about 4.5 h; 3S1P -> about 2.3 h [INFERRED]. Stall of both 25D HP motors adds 110 W for the seconds until the ESP32 current limit trips; the pack sees 10 A plus 1 A, fine for 2P (16 A) and marginal for 1P (8 A).

**Telemetry and low-battery behavior.** INA226 high-side at the battery main lead, external 2 mOhm 3 W shunt (40 A full scale, 0.6 mA LSB with 16-bit), averaging 64-128 samples, 1 ms conversion, read at 10 Hz by the ESP32-S3; expose pack V, I, and coulomb count over the existing serial protocol. Thresholds under load (pack voltage, per-cell in brackets), each requiring 10 s continuous: 10.5 V (3.5) -> Pi says "battery low"; 9.9 V (3.3) -> ESP32 refuses drive commands, Pi says so; 9.6 V (3.2) -> ESP32 requests Pi shutdown and cuts the Pi rail 60 s later or when `gpio-poweroff` fires. Suspend the drive-inhibit check while motor current exceeds 2 A to avoid tripping on stall sag. The BMS's 2.5-3.0 V/cell cutoff is the last line only.

**Thermal.** Pi 4 in the enclosure: 25 mm or larger finned heatsink on the SoC plus a 30 or 40 mm 5 V fan (60-100 mA) on the Pi rail, `dtoverlay=gpio-fan,gpiopin=14,temp=60000` (on at 60 C, off at 50 C), intake low near the drivers and exhaust high. Target under the full voice loop: SoC <70 C, `vcgencmd get_throttled` = 0x0 after 30 min. Log `vcgencmd measure_temp`, `measure_temp pmic`, and `measure_clock arm` every 10 s in gate 3 and gate 4; do not use sysfs `scaling_cur_freq` for throttling detection [MEASURED, Rowan]. Decoding: bits 0/16 under-voltage now/since boot, 1/17 Arm frequency capped, 2/18 throttled, 3/19 soft limit; on Pi 4 bits 3/19 never set because no soft limit is defined [VENDOR]. Motor drivers and the 5 A buck need airflow more than the ESP32-S3 does; at 12 W total the enclosure only needs a few CFM.

**Clean shutdown.** config.txt: `dtoverlay=gpio-shutdown,gpio_pin=17,active_low=0,gpio_pull=down` and `dtoverlay=gpio-poweroff,gpiopin=26`. ESP32 drives GPIO 17 high to request shutdown, watches GPIO 26 for the poweroff pulse train, then opens a P-MOSFET (or the buck's EN pin: D36V50F5 EN pulls the regulator below 1 mA) on the Pi rail. A momentary button on the ESP32 does the same for humans. Do not leave the Pi rail on after halt if the pack is low: halt draws 23-69 mA [MEASURED]. Skip UPS HATs; the ESP32 already is the power supervisor.

## Corrections to the brief

1. "5 V/5 A buck (Pi) $20". A regulator that holds the Pi 4 above 4.8 V through motor transients costs $33-40 (Pololu D24V50F5/D36V50F5) [VENDOR]. The $10-20 LM2596/XL4015 class is the one with 5.05-5.2 V sag at 1.3 A and about 1 A real continuous capability [MEASURED]. The LeKiwi BOM's $9.99 "12 V to 5 V 5 A USB-C converter" is the budget option that ecosystem uses; quality is unverified. Budget $35.
2. "GPIO 5 V injection bypasses input fuse". The Pi 4 has no input polyfuse; the USB-C input has TVS overvoltage/reverse protection only. Header injection bypasses that protection, so add a 3 A polyfuse and TVS yourself [forum consensus; official docs say back-powering "bypasses the protection circuitry"]. The tradeoff is real but it is about protection, not a fuse.
3. Gate 3 "no get_throttled flags". Holds, with two amendments: on Pi 4 bits 3/19 cannot set (no soft limit) so the flags that matter are 0x1/0x10000 and 0x4/0x40000; and frequency must be read with `vcgencmd measure_clock arm`, since sysfs showed 1500 MHz while the board was throttled [MEASURED, Rowan].
4. "5.1 V/3 A official spec, undervoltage ~4.63 V": holds exactly (3.0 A recommended, 1.2 A USB, 600 mA bare board, 4.63 V +/-5 %) [VENDOR].
5. "Pi 4 throttling thresholds (80/85 C)": holds; add that `temp_limit` cannot be raised above 85 and that Pi 4 has no soft limit [VENDOR].
6. "Motor driver $8-15" and "3S Li-ion": fine together, but if the chosen driver is TB6612FNG (13.5 V max, 1 A continuous) it rules out 4S LiFePO4 and 25D HP motors (5 A stall). Pick DRV8871 x2 ($15) unless motors are JGA25 class.
7. Gate 2 "e-stop kills motor rail with everything else off": read as "e-stop cuts only the motor rail while Pi and ESP32 stay powered". Take the Pi buck's input upstream of the e-stop or the Pi reboots on every e-stop and the 300 ms TTL logic never gets tested under a real stop.
8. "3S Li-ion pack + BMS + charger $40-55": holds ($33 pack class + $10-15 charger, or about $45-60 for 6 quality cells, BMS, holder, and charger) [VENDOR].
9. Gate 4 "all-on thermal/power OK": add explicit pass criteria: header voltage >=5.0 V during a both-motors stall, `get_throttled` 0x0, SoC <70 C after 30 min in the closed enclosure. Bare-board data says an unventilated box will not pass at 3-4 cores [MEASURED, INFERRED for the 2-core case].

## Alternatives considered and rejected

- **4S LiFePO4.** Safer chemistry and 2000+ cycles, but 14.6 V full exceeds TB6612FNG's 13.5 V, needs a LiFePO4 BMS and 14.6 V charger, weighs and costs more per Wh, and its flat curve forces coulomb counting for state of charge. Rejected; revisit if the rover lives on a charger 24/7 and cycle life dominates.
- **3S LiPo.** Lighter and cheaper for the same Ah, but no integrated BMS, needs a balance charger every cycle, and puffing/fire risk is worse for a robot that sits in a living room. Rejected.
- **Two-battery architecture** (Pi on a USB-PD power bank or a UPS HAT such as Geekworm X728 / Waveshare UPS HAT (E), motors on the 12 V pack). LeKiwi's 5 V version does this and it isolates the Pi from motor noise completely. Rejected for weight, two charge ports, the bank's low-current auto-off, and because the ESP32 already supervises power; keep it as the fallback if brown-outs persist after wiring fixes.
- **LM2596/XL4015 modules for the Pi.** Measured sag and thermal limits above. Rejected; acceptable only for the ESP32/sensor rail at <0.5 A.
- **Powering the Pi through the e-stop.** Simplest wiring, but the safety chain must be testable with the Pi alive. Rejected.
- **Analog 3.5 mm audio out or a USB DAC into an analog amp.** More ground paths, more hum. Rejected in favor of I2S MAX98357A.
- **Voltage-only low-battery logic with no current sensing.** Motor stall sag would false-trigger; the INA226 gives current for debounce and a coulomb counter for free. Rejected.

## Open questions

- Which motors? The stall current (1.8 A vs 5 A each) sets the fuse, the driver, and whether 1P is acceptable.
- Enclosure geometry and where the fan can exhaust; no measurement exists for this specific box, so gate 4 must log temperature and clocks.
- Does the USB mic pick up hum with the fan and motors running? Only a test on the chassis answers this; the fallback (USB isolator or mic on the ESP32) is cheap.
- Will the Pi stay halted on the rail (23-69 mA) or be fully cut? The gpio-poweroff route needs the P-MOSFET or EN-pin wiring decided before the harness is built.
- Cell cutoff of the chosen BMS (2.5 vs 3.0 V/cell) and its balance current; cheap boards balance at tens of mA and may never catch up on a 7 Ah pack.
- Whether the "12 V 5 A" prebuilt pack's BMS trips on a 10 A stall; its continuous rating is unknown.
- The brief's 5 V-rail current under the actual voice workload is inferred (0.9-1.1 A); measure it with the INA226 on the Pi rail during gate 3.

### Stale or missing

- **Stale thermal and power baselines.** Every measured Pi 4 figure in the note (Rowan Sept 2019, raspi.tv 2019, the official June 2019 current table) predates the Nov 2019 firmware fix (idle 2.89 -> 2.1 W, load 7.28 -> 6.41 W per Raspberry Pi's own blog), the Bullseye-era `arm_boost=1` 1.8 GHz default on rev 1.4+ boards, and the Nov 2024 firmware change to `initial_turbo=60` with the `ondemand` governor. Two effects pull in opposite directions; the only safe statement is "re-measure on this board with the installed image," which the note's gate 3/4 logging does, but the summary sentence "official Pi 4 numbers hold in 2026" overstates it.
- **DVFS step table** omits the 1800 MHz top step for boosted boards. Check with `vcgencmd get_config arm_freq` and `vcgencmd measure_clock arm` at load.
- **GPIO 14 fan pin vs UART0.** `gpio-fan,gpiopin=14` copies the official Case Fan default, but GPIO 14 is UART0 TXD. The brief's ESP32 link is "Serial to Pi"; if that is the header UART the fan breaks it. Pick a free GPIO for the fan or use the ESP32-S3's native USB-CDC (which also removes the 3.3 V level-shift question).
- **DRV8871 thermal math is 2x pessimistic** (565 mOhm is HS+LS). Not dangerous, but the "4.5 W at 2 A" figure would wrongly push the design toward a bigger driver or heatsink.
- **INA226 resolution** with a 2 mOhm shunt is 1.25 mA/LSB, not 0.6 mA. Coulomb counting at 10 Hz with 64-128 averages is still ample.
- **Samsung 35E cutoff** is 2.65 V in Samsung's sheet, and an independent tester limits sustained draw to ~10 A/cell. Firmware cutoffs of 3.2-3.3 V make this moot, but the 3S1P option puts ~11 A through one cell during a double 25D HP stall, above the 8 A continuous rating; say "3S1P only with JGA25-class motors."
- **XT30/XT60 numbers** quoted are hobby burst ratings; Amass-rated continuous is 15 A (XT30) and 30 A (XT60).
- **Runtime target not in the brief.** The note says a 3S1P pack "still meets the 1-2 h target"; the brief states no runtime target (only a 30 min continuous gate). Either the target came from elsewhere or it should be marked as the note's own assumption.
- **Silent BOM drift vs the brief.** The note's preferred parts exceed the brief's line items without saying so: Pololu 25D HP motors $113.90/pair vs "$50-100" for motors + chassis + caster; Pololu VL53L1X carriers $45.90/pair vs "$15-20" for 2x VL53L1X + bumper + e-stop; Pi buck $33-40 vs $20 (this one is flagged). With the note's picks the "$200-300 beyond the Pi" total lands at the top or above; the note should either name cheaper VL53L1X breakouts/JGA25 motors as the baseline or restate the total.
- **LeKiwi comparison is a Pi 5 BOM.** The note presents LeKiwi's "12 V to 5 V 5 A USB-C converter" as the ecosystem default without noting it is sized for a Pi 5's 5 A PD input, which weakens it as a Pi 4 precedent.
- **Motor-rail energy when the rail opens.** A 2026 practitioner would insist on a bulk capacitor and TVS on the motor-driver side of the e-stop (and behind the BMS): when the NC e-stop opens or the BMS discharge FET trips while motors are spinning, the DRV8871s brake/regenerate into a rail with no battery to absorb it. Also specify a common-port BMS if the pack charges through the same leads it discharges through.
- **Cell holders.** The "6-cell holder" option is a poor fit at 5-10 A pack current; spring holders add tens of mOhm and lose contact under vibration. Spot-weld or use rigid PCB-mount holders.
- **Pi 4-specific voltage telemetry.** The Aug 2026 wiring-drop datapoint relied on Pi 5's `vcgencmd pmic_read_adc`; Pi 4 cannot read its own 5 V input. The INA226-on-Pi-rail plan in the open questions should be promoted to a gate 3 requirement so the ">=5.0 V at the header" pass criterion is actually measurable.
- **Availability.** Pololu lists the D24V50F5 as "Rationed (Active)"; order early or plan on the D36V50F5.
